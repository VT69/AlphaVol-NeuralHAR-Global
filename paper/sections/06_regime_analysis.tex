\section{Regime-Conditional Analysis}
\label{sec:regime}
To investigate whether the marginal predictive content of sentiment is state-dependent, we filter the data into low, medium, and high volatility regimes using a Hidden Markov Model. 

Table \ref{tab:regime_coefs} presents the regime-conditional in-sample coefficients for the HAR-S model. Contrary to the hypothesis that sentiment is most impactful during specific market stress periods, the FinBERT sentiment coefficients are statistically insignificant across all regimes for both SPX ($p \approx 0.17 - 0.55$) and NIFTY ($p \approx 0.58 - 0.93$). Furthermore, for BTC, the order-book imbalance (OBI) metric exhibits strong significance in the low and medium regimes ($p < 0.01$), but loses statistical significance in the high-stress regime ($p=0.12$). Regime-conditional OOS forecast accuracy is provided in Table~\ref{tab:regime_oos}. Ultimately, we do not find robust evidence that NLP-derived sentiment's marginal predictive content is strictly regime-dependent.
